# Contributions and Attribution

## Scope

This portfolio describes my individual work within a collaborative UNSW Battlecode 2026 project. It is not a claim of sole authorship of the team's bot, shared codebase, or experimental infrastructure.

## Attribution Principles

For each investigation, I distinguish between:

- **Problem formulation:** identifying or proposing the behaviour to investigate.
- **Strategy design:** specifying the intended policy and its constraints.
- **Implementation:** writing or modifying the code.
- **Experimental work:** configuring and running tests, collecting measurements, and inspecting replays.
- **Analysis:** interpreting the results and identifying limitations.
- **Integration:** reviewing, merging, or deploying a candidate into the shared bot.

These responsibilities can belong to different contributors. A public issue, commit, or pull request is evidence of a particular activity, but is not by itself proof that one person performed every part of the work.

## AI-Assisted Development

AI tools were used in the broader development workflow. Where relevant, the case studies distinguish my own decisions and verified work from implementation or analysis assisted by AI.

AI assistance is not treated as evidence that I independently authored all generated code. Conversely, using AI assistance does not erase personal contributions such as defining a problem, making design decisions, validating behaviour, or interpreting experimental results.

## Shared Code and Publication

The relevant shared source code, test artifacts, maps, and replays are approved for public release and may be included in this portfolio.

The repository presents these materials as part of a collaborative project. Public availability does not imply sole authorship: individual responsibilities are attributed using issues, commits, pull requests, test records, and other available evidence.

## Evidence Standard

Claims about personal contributions should be traceable to relevant source changes, issue discussions, experimental records, or integration work. Where a result reflects team-wide work, it is described as a collaborative outcome rather than an individual achievement.
